BBA Partnership Audit Rules: Election Out, Representatives, and Push-Out Review

Start with the reviewed year, partner roster, filed election, and actual IRS notices before choosing a response.

Published July 24, 2026 • Reviewed November 22, 2026 • AE Tax Advisors

The BBA centralized partnership audit regime generally applies to tax years beginning after 2017 unless a valid election or other applicable rule changes the procedure. It can lead to a partnership-level imputed underpayment even though ordinary partnership income otherwise passes through to partners. An LLC’s name or size alone does not establish its federal tax classification or election-out eligibility.

Start with the reviewed tax year, the filed return, the partner roster, and any actual IRS notice. Download the blank BBA review record and fictional election-out counting examples. These records help organize a review; they do not make an election, establish a deadline, or calculate a final tax liability.

Election out is an annual eligibility and filing review

An eligible partnership generally can elect out on its timely filed return, including extensions. The election must be made for the relevant year; it does not automatically carry forward. Review the applicable Form 1065 election question, Schedule B-2, required information, and partner notification rules. Do not assume that a current eligible roster proves an older year’s election was valid.

Eligible partner types include individuals, C corporations, qualifying foreign entities that would be C corporations if domestic, S corporations, and estates of deceased partners. Partnerships, trusts, disregarded entities, certain other foreign entities and estates, and persons holding an interest on another’s behalf are ineligible under the IRS rules. A single-member disregarded LLC partner requires attention even if its owner is an individual.

The 100-or-fewer count is not just a headcount of direct owners. Add the Schedules K-1 the partnership must issue and the Schedules K-1 its S-corporation partners must issue to their shareholders for the relevant S-corporation tax years. The direct K-1 issued to the S corporation is already part of the first number; the shareholder K-1s add to it.

Know who can act for the partnership

A partnership subject to BBA generally designates a partnership representative for each tax year. The representative needs substantial U.S. presence; an entity representative also requires a designated individual. The representative has sole authority to act for BBA procedures, and its actions can bind the partnership and partners.

An agreement can set internal consent, notice, document-access, reimbursement, and indemnification duties. Those private arrangements should not be mistaken for restrictions on the representative’s authority before the IRS. Review how former reviewed-year partners cooperate and how the economic burden is allocated when ownership has changed. Keep those contract questions separate from the IRS’s collection rules.

An imputed underpayment is more than a guessed tax bracket

The calculation involves grouping, subgrouping, and netting partnership adjustments, the highest applicable section 1 or 11 rate for the reviewed year, and separate treatment of credit and creditable-expenditure adjustments. It is not automatically the total of every adjustment multiplied by today’s rate. Negative adjustments do not necessarily offset positive adjustments across groups.

Isolated fictional arithmetic: assume a supported $200,000 total netted partnership adjustment and an applicable 37% reviewed-year rate, with no credit-group additions or approved modifications. The product is $74,000 before interest and penalties. That is not a final bill. Interest, penalties, modification eligibility, partner information, and a valid push-out choice remain unresolved. A partner’s lower ordinary bracket alone is not evidence that a particular reduction is available or approved.

Keep the audit push-out election and statements separate

For an audit adjustment, the IRS describes a 45-day election period running from the date of the final partnership adjustment notice, not simply the date someone opens the mail. This period cannot be extended. The representative uses Form 8988 with the required reviewed-year partner information. Verify the actual notice, dates, and available procedures with the responsible professional rather than calculating an election deadline from this article.

After the election, the IRS instructs the partnership to wait for the countersigned Form 8988 before submitting push-out forms. The audited partnership furnishes Form 8986 to reviewed-year partners and submits Form 8985 with the statements to the IRS. The separate 60-day statement period runs from when the audit matters become final under the applicable rules; it is not simply another period beginning on the FPA date. Review court proceedings and finality before recording the due date.

These statements are not ordinary amended K-1s issued by partners. Non-pass-through recipients generally use Form 8978 with the reporting-year return to report the effect. Pass-through partners have separate further-push-out or payment procedures and deadlines. Section 6226 also changes the applicable interest calculation, including a two-percentage-point rate increase. A push-out is not a promise of lower combined tax, interest, or administration cost.

A return correction is a different decision

A BBA partnership correcting a filed return generally reviews the administrative adjustment request process rather than assuming an ordinary amended return with amended K-1s is available. An AAR has its own timing, payment, and statement rules; an audit push-out election is not the same procedure. The IRS says an AAR cannot be filed after a notice of administrative proceeding has been issued for the year. Review the current instructions and the actual procedural stage before filing.

Prepare the review file

  1. Identify the tax classification, reviewed year, filed return, and evidence of any annual election out.
  2. Reconcile the direct and S-corporation shareholder K-1 counts and every partner’s eligible type.
  3. Confirm the representative and any designated individual, and locate the agreement’s internal cooperation provisions.
  4. Preserve the complete notice and envelope, record notice dates, and have a professional verify each applicable deadline.
  5. Compare supported partnership payment, modification, and push-out consequences, including partner information, interest, penalties, and administration.

Continue with the Schedule K-1 reading guide and special-allocation review. This guide does not estimate the likelihood of an audit or recommend an entity change to avoid scrutiny.
