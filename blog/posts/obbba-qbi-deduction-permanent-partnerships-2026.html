Partnership QBI Deduction: 2026 Limits and a Partner Review Worksheet

Follow the K-1 information through qualified income and return-level limits before estimating tax savings.

Published July 3, 2026 • Reviewed November 22, 2026 • AE Tax Advisors

The qualified business income deduction is calculated on an eligible owner’s return, using qualified items and return-level limits. A partnership distribution is not QBI, a Schedule K-1 profit figure is not automatically the final QBI amount, and a 20% deduction is not a 20% cash tax saving.

This guide follows the records from partnership profit to the partner’s section 199A review. Use the blank QBI review record and fictional partner reconciliation examples to separate supported inputs from unresolved conclusions.

What changed after 2025

The 2025 law removed section 199A’s scheduled expiration and expanded the phase-in intervals for tax years beginning after 2025. Current law has no scheduled sunset for the deduction. That does not guarantee an owner’s future deduction, tax rate, or eligibility; the applicable rules and each year’s facts still control.

For 2026, the interval is $150,000 for joint returns and $75,000 for other returns. The older intervals were $100,000 and $50,000 respectively. Do not use a $150,000 single-filer interval or substitute the top income-tax bracket threshold for the section 199A threshold.

Use the correct 2026 threshold and endpoint

Revenue Procedure 2025-32, section 4.26, gives the following amounts. Test taxable income before the QBI deduction, rather than partnership revenue, AGI, cash distributions, or a partner’s share alone.

Below the applicable threshold, the wage/property and specified-service restrictions generally do not limit the business component, but qualified-income and overall taxable-income rules still matter. Within the interval, limitations phase in. Above the endpoint, a specified service trade or business generally does not supply a qualified business component under those rules; an otherwise qualifying non-SSTB business remains subject to the fully applicable wage/property limitation.

Reconcile the K-1 statement to the partner’s qualified items

Obtain the section 199A supporting statement associated with K-1 box 20, code Z, and identify each business or reported aggregation. The statement supplies information for the owner’s calculation, not a partnership-approved deduction. Qualifying income and deductions, prior losses, wages, unadjusted basis immediately after acquisition of qualified property, and the business’s activity must be reconciled.

Service guaranteed payments are excluded from the recipient’s QBI. Their deductible expense can already reduce partnership income and the QBI allocated to partners. If the starting profit already reflects the payment, do not subtract it again. Qualified income can also need partner-level adjustments, including properly allocable deductible self-employment tax, self-employed health insurance, and retirement contributions. Review the actual deduction and allocation rather than assuming the gross K-1 amount is QBI.

Capital gains, most dividends, and investment interest generally are outside QBI, subject to the applicable definitions and exceptions. Guaranteed payments for capital and other partner transactions need their own analysis. Cash distributions and a change in the compensation label do not themselves establish a qualified item. See the corrected guaranteed-payment guide before comparing economic arrangements.

Apply the business and return-level limits

When fully applicable, the wage/property limit uses the greater of 50% of qualifying W-2 wages or 25% of those wages plus 2.5% of qualifying property’s UBIA. A partner’s guaranteed payment is not automatically a qualifying W-2 wage. SSTB classification, aggregation, qualified property, loss carryforwards, qualified REIT/PTP components, and cooperative rules can change the calculation. Document their applicability before comparing entities or changing an agreement.

The general overall cap is 20% × (taxable income before the QBI deduction − net capital gain, including qualified dividends), with the applicable definitions and floor at zero. This is distinct from 20% of QBI. Section 199A(i) also has a special $400 minimum for an applicable taxpayer with at least $1,000 aggregate QBI from qualifying active businesses in which the taxpayer materially participates, subject to its rules. Neither ownership alone nor receiving a K-1 establishes that condition.

Three fictional partner review cases

Assume one qualifying domestic business has $150,000 ordinary income before a fully deductible $60,000 service guaranteed payment. Remaining income is $90,000. The fictional recipient has a valid 50% share of that remainder, or $45,000, plus the separately reported $60,000 service payment. The $45,000 profit share already reflects the partnership’s payment deduction. Do not subtract $60,000 from it again.

In cases A and B, assume the recipient has $5,000 of supported, properly allocable partner-level QBI deductions, making QBI $40,000. This $5,000 is a supplied fictional input, not a calculated SE-tax deduction or recommended contribution. Both cases assume a single filer, no other business or carryforward, no REIT/PTP or cooperative component, and taxable income below the single threshold. Eligibility is assumed only to illustrate arithmetic. Taxable income is a supplied whole-return figure after other items and deductions; it is not the sum of the payment and profit share.

Case B’s cap is ($110,000 − $80,000) × 20% = $6,000, below its $8,000 business component. Case C leaves the adjustment and deduction blank instead of treating missing information as zero. All cases leave tax savings unresolved. They do not compare a guaranteed payment with an alternative profit allocation or establish an actual filing position.

Complete the review before using a savings number

  1. Record tax year, filing status, and the whole return’s taxable income before the deduction.
  2. Collect each section 199A statement, reconcile already-deducted payments, and support partner-level adjustments.
  3. Resolve the business’s qualification, SSTB status, wage/property data, carryforwards, and any special components.
  4. Calculate the business component and overall cap separately; review any applicable minimum-deduction rule.
  5. Calculate the whole-return tax effect separately from the deduction. Do not multiply an unsupported deduction by a guessed bracket and promise recurring savings.

Keep QBI loss carryforwards distinct from losses suspended under basis, at-risk, or passive rules; release and QBI inclusion can depend on when the underlying loss becomes allowable. Continue with the corrected outside-basis guide and the Schedule K-1 reading guide.
