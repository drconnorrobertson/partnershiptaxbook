Section 754 Election: Transfers, Basis Adjustments and Review

Separate the election, transaction, basis computation and asset-level follow-through.

Published June 29, 2026 · Updated November 21, 2026 · AE Tax Advisors

A section 754 election is a partnership-level decision about basis adjustments. It is not a guaranteed tax saving, an immediate deduction for the price of an interest, or a way to reset every partner’s asset basis. Start with the transaction, existing election history and supporting basis schedules before estimating its effect.

Separate the election from the adjustment

Section 754 connects two adjustment regimes: section 743 for transfers of interests and section 734 for distributions. An election applies to relevant transactions in the election year and subsequent years, subject to revocation rules. Do not model only the favorable transfer currently in front of the owners.

Identify which transaction is being reviewed
EventReview frameworkRecord to prepare
Sale, exchange or transfer at death of a partnership interestSection 743 basis-adjustment reviewTransfer documents, transferee outside basis and partnership asset information
Distribution by the partnershipSection 734 basis-adjustment reviewDistribution records, partner and property basis, and any recognized gain or loss

A transfer can produce an increase or decrease

Section 743 describes a transferee-specific adjustment based on the difference between the transferee’s interest basis and the relevant proportionate share of partnership property basis. The result can be positive or negative. It also requires adjustment in certain substantial-built-in-loss cases without an election; the absence of a section 754 statement does not end the analysis.

For a fictional comparison, assume the reviewer has already determined the relevant outside basis and proportionate share under the applicable rules. These are inputs to illustrate subtraction, not a method for determining the inputs:

Input or resultIncrease exampleDecrease example
Transferee interest basis$250,000$40,000
Relevant proportionate share of property basis$60,000$60,000
Difference+$190,000−$20,000

The $190,000 difference is not $190,000 of cash, an automatic current deduction or a calculated tax saving. The negative case is not optional merely because the owners would prefer an increase. Determining whether and how an adjustment applies requires the complete transaction review.

The asset allocation controls what happens next

Section 755 governs allocation among partnership properties. An aggregate adjustment does not establish that every dollar belongs to a depreciable building or has the same recovery treatment. Prepare asset-level values, tax bases and character information, then have the preparer determine the allocation and subsequent schedules. Do not multiply the aggregate by a single capital-gains rate and label it assured savings.

For example, the fictional $190,000 figure above might be marked “aggregate difference reconciled; asset allocation pending; timing and tax character not determined.” That record shows why a purchase-price calculation alone cannot establish the result on the next return. Ask the reviewer to connect each assigned amount to its asset and reporting treatment.

A distribution requires its own workpaper

Section 734 concerns basis adjustments to undistributed partnership property following certain distributions, with both increase and decrease rules. Certain substantial basis reductions can require adjustment without an election. Do not reuse the transferee-only section 743 schedule as if the two events were identical.

Collect what was distributed, when it moved, the relevant partner and property bases, and any gain or loss calculation. Record missing valuation or liability information before assigning an adjustment to remaining assets. A description such as “owner exit” does not tell the preparer whether the transaction was an interest sale, redemption, property distribution or another arrangement.

Review timing and continuing obligations before filing

Treasury Regulation 1.754-1 addresses the written election statement, the partnership return and timely filing including applicable extensions. A valid existing election generally does not need to be remade each year. It applies to both adjustment regimes, and revocation requires the applicable approval process. If timing was missed, ask the preparer to evaluate available relief and its conditions promptly rather than assuming relief is automatic or routinely granted.

  • Transaction identity: collect the executed transfer or distribution documents and a chronology. Identify which facts remain proposed rather than completed.
  • Election history: locate the actual filed statement and return, not just a checkbox in an internal spreadsheet. Ask whether any valid revocation changed the history.
  • Basis reconciliation: separate purchase consideration, liabilities, outside basis and asset bases. Preserve the explanation for every adjustment.
  • Asset-level follow-through: assign responsibility for valuation support, allocation, recovery schedules and later disposition records.
  • Future administration: compare the continuing tracking work, possible decreases and other transactions with the owners’ expected ownership changes.

Keep the decision file usable after closing

Use a register with the item, supporting document, reviewer, open question and next action. “Election located,” “adjustment computed” and “asset allocation reviewed” are separate milestones. A new owner or preparer should be able to trace the final schedules to the same completed transaction without recreating the assumptions from a promotional estimate.
