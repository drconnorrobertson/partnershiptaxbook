What Is Schedule K-1? Partnership Boxes and Review Checklist

Identify the form, reconcile the records and review the applicable limitations.

Published June 29, 2026 · Updated November 21, 2026 · AE Tax Advisors

Schedule K-1 reports an owner’s share of items from a pass-through entity. It is not a receipt showing only cash paid to that owner, and it does not by itself calculate the owner’s complete tax liability. Start by identifying the issuing form and tax year, then retain the complete package and supporting records for the preparer’s review.

Identify the K-1 before using a box reference

This guide focuses on Schedule K-1 (Form 1065) for a partnership. An S corporation uses Form 1120-S’s K-1; an estate or trust uses Form 1041’s K-1. Do not transfer the box map from one to another. The map below follows the IRS’s currently published 2025 partnership form and instructions, checked November 21, 2026. Use the version applicable to the return being prepared.

The partner’s instructions explain the codes, attached statements, reporting and limitations. A box number alone is not enough to select a return line or a tax rate. Obtain missing statements before treating the package as complete, including the information needed for any QBI, foreign or multiple-activity review that applies.

Keep allocated income and cash distributions separate

IRS Publication 541 explains that partnership items pass through to partners and that distributions have separate basis consequences. Cash exceeding the relevant adjusted outside basis can create gain. A distribution is not automatically tax-free merely because it is called an owner draw.

For a simplified fictional example, assume a partner has $80,000 of adjusted outside basis immediately before a $100,000 cash distribution, with no other relevant adjustments or exceptions. The cash exceeds that basis by $20,000; that excess generally creates gain under the section 731 cash-distribution rule. This is not a complete calculation for a real transaction. Liability changes, property distributions and other facts require review.

In another fictional case, a partner receives an allocation of income but no cash. The absence of a distribution does not establish that the allocation is untaxed. Reconcile the tax allocation and the actual money separately, then ask how the owner will fund any resulting tax payment.

A reported loss is not automatically a usable deduction

If an invented loss is $40,000 and the applicable basis limitation is $25,000, the section 704(d) basis test alone cannot support deducting the full $40,000. It also does not establish that $25,000 is currently deductible: at-risk, passive-activity and other applicable rules may further limit the result. Keep suspended amounts and their supporting schedules from year to year.

Do not substitute the capital account shown on the K-1 for a complete outside-basis schedule. Use the outside-basis tracking guide to prepare the contribution, distribution and liability records. The special-allocation review explains why an allocation’s bookkeeping and tax validity require separate attention.

Review self-employment information without applying a blanket rate

Box 14 is a review input, not an instruction to add 15.3% and then another 2.9% to every amount. The Schedule SE instructions address partnership earnings, the limited-partner rule and coordination with other earnings. The preparer needs the applicable income character, owner facts and tax-year limits. An LLC or limited-partner label alone does not settle the answer.

Build a K-1 intake and reconciliation register

  • Inventory the package: record issuer, tax year, form type, original or corrected status, receipt date and which statements are present. Keep identifiers and tax documents in a secure system.
  • Match the ownership record: flag changes in interests, contributions, transfers or exits. Give the preparer the relevant agreement and transaction records rather than assuming the prior year still applies.
  • Reconcile actual cash: match distributions and contributions to the owner’s records. Keep reimbursements, loans and payments for services separately identified for review.
  • Track unanswered questions: identify the box, code or statement, the source of the discrepancy, the person asked and the next-action date. Do not alter the issuer’s form to make it match an assumption.
  • Retain the conclusion: keep the preparer’s mapping, limitation schedules and explanation of any correction or filing position alongside the final package.

A fictional register might show “K-1 received; box 20 statement missing; requested from issuer; tax calculation pending.” That is more precise than “K-1 complete.” When a replacement package arrives, preserve version history and tell the preparer which figures changed instead of silently overwriting the earlier copy.

Plan for missing or late information

Ask the preparer to confirm the applicable filing and payment deadlines and how to proceed if a package is late or incorrect. An individual extension to file does not extend the time to pay tax. A missing K-1 still calls for a supported payment estimate and a clear plan; do not assume a partnership extension automatically extends the owner’s obligations.
