Section 754 Election: Partnership Basis Adjustments Explained

By AE Tax Advisors | Updated October 1, 2026

Understand when a partnership basis adjustment may matter after an ownership transfer or distribution, with an illustrative buyer example.

What the election changes

A buyer can pay more for a partnership interest than the share of tax basis associated with the partnership's assets. The buyer's outside basis and the partnership's inside basis can therefore differ. A Section 754 election permits adjustments under Section 743(b) following qualifying transfers and under Section 734(b) following qualifying distributions. The election is made by the partnership, not unilaterally by the incoming partner.

A simplified buyer example

Assume a buyer pays $300,000 for an interest and the relevant share of adjusted asset basis is $180,000. Ignore liabilities and other complications. The $120,000 difference illustrates a potential positive adjustment. A proper Section 743(b) calculation, asset allocation and recovery analysis are still required. It is not automatically an immediate deduction.

The adjustment following an interest transfer generally relates to the transferee partner. It should not be described as a $120,000 increase available equally to all partners. The assets receiving the adjustment affect the timing and character of the tax consequences.

Inside basis, outside basis and capital accounts

Outside basis measures the partner's investment for tax purposes. Inside basis concerns the partnership's assets. Capital accounts measure a different set of economic or reporting information. Substituting a capital-account balance for the required tax-basis calculation can produce the wrong adjustment.

Making and maintaining the election

The IRS describes attaching the election statement to the timely filed partnership return, including extensions, for the relevant year. The election generally continues for later years unless properly revoked. Mandatory adjustments can apply in certain loss situations even without an election. A missed election or proposed revocation needs its own review rather than an assumption that a bookkeeping entry fixes the issue.

Prepare the transaction file

  • Purchase agreement, ownership percentages and transaction date.
  • Consideration, liabilities and the buyer's outside-basis calculation.
  • Asset tax basis, valuation and proposed allocation of the adjustment.
  • Prior elections, current return deadline and continuing adjustment schedules.

Before closing, ask who will calculate the adjustment, obtain valuations and maintain the resulting schedules. A transaction price alone does not show the annual deductions or ultimate tax benefit. Compare the benefit with ongoing reporting costs and the expected holding period.
