Hiring Help to Correct a Partnership Return: AAR Engagement Checklist

If a profitable business or property partnership discovers a return error, the first purchase decision is not simply which preparer offers the lowest amendment fee. The engagement must identify the correct correction procedure, the people authorized to act, and the entity and partner deliverables. This guide helps an owner compare proposals before paying for work.

Start with the return and the error, not a promised refund

Tell the adviser which tax year is involved, when the return was filed, what appears wrong, and whether any IRS notice has arrived. A missing depreciation adjustment, an incorrect ownership allocation and an omitted income item may require different analysis. Ask for a written issue inventory that distinguishes confirmed errors from questions still awaiting records.

For partnership-related items, a partnership subject to the BBA regime generally uses an administrative adjustment request rather than an ordinary amended return. Whether the partnership validly elected out must be established for the reviewed year. Do not infer it from the number of owners or the fact that everyone received a Schedule K-1. The IRS AAR guide describes the filing route and authority requirements.

What a useful proposal should separate

  • Diagnosis: review of the filed return, election status, transaction records and relevant deadlines.
  • Entity calculations: corrected items, supporting schedules and the required imputed-underpayment analysis.
  • Filing package: preparation, authorized signature, submission and acceptance tracking.
  • Partner handoff: statements, explanations and coordination with each affected owner's preparer.
  • State review: a separate list of state consequences and who is engaged to handle them.

Request a fee for each included workstream or a clear combined scope. Ask what triggers a revised quote: incomplete books, additional years, tiered owners, replacement basis schedules, notices or state filings. A low quoted filing fee may exclude the calculations that make the filing usable.

Who is authorized to act?

Identify the reviewed-year partnership representative and any designated individual before scheduling signatures. Ownership alone does not make every partner the authorized AAR signer. Ask the adviser to verify the designation against the filed return and any later valid change. If partners have conflicting economic interests, the engagement should explain whom the adviser represents and when separate counsel or separate tax advice is appropriate.

Use a responsibility list with a named owner for records, calculations, review, signature and delivery. This is especially important when the partnership's current owners differ from the owners in the year being corrected.

Do not confuse a corrected number with a completed correction

A BBA correction can involve an entity payment or partner-level reporting, depending on the adjustment and elections. Ask the adviser to explain the proposed route before submission, including which owners bear the work and when they must act. The engagement should not promise that every favorable adjustment produces an immediate cash refund.

Where the procedure requires Form 8986, sending an amended K-1 instead is not a substitute. Request a partner-by-partner delivery list and specify whether the adviser also handles downstream reporting or only provides the partnership package. The Form 1065-X instructions and Form 8082 instructions should be checked for the applicable filing method and year.

Records to organize before the first paid review

  • Original filed returns, all K-1s and proof of filing or extension.
  • Operating agreement, amendments and ownership-change dates.
  • Books, source documents and schedules relevant to the suspected error.
  • Prior correction filings, IRS correspondence and representative designations.
  • Partner contact and preparer coordination information, exchanged through a secure channel.

Describe missing records honestly. A professional may offer a limited diagnostic phase before agreeing to the complete correction. That can be more useful than a fixed-price promise built on assumptions about unavailable documents.

Compare completion evidence

Before hiring, agree on what you receive at the end: filed forms, acceptance evidence where available, a reconciliation to the original return, partner-delivery records and a list of outstanding actions. Electronic acceptance is not an IRS determination that the tax position is correct. Keep the underlying workpapers and the explanation of unresolved issues.

If the filing deadline or an examination notice could matter, request a dated assessment rather than waiting for a routine onboarding cycle. Do not assume a refund deadline, AAR window and state reporting deadline are identical.
